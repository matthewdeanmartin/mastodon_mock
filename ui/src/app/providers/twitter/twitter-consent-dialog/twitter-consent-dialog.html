What could go wrong

If {{ proxy().label }} logged or misused what passes through it, whoever holds your key could:

  • Spend your credits. {{ source().label }} bills per request, so a stolen key draws down the balance you paid for until you notice and rotate it.
  • Look up any public Twitter account, post or search using your account's quota

And separately from the key itself: because every X request goes through the proxy, its operator can see which accounts you read and what you search for. That is your reading history, not something you published.

What this does not cover

Only your {{ source().label }} key and the Twitter content you look up. Mawkingbird still refuses to send your Mastodon session, your Bluesky account, or any other connected service through a proxy. No Twitter account password or session cookie is involved — this app never asks for one, and reading public Twitter data does not require it.

If you would rather not take this risk, you can run your own proxy and select it under CORS proxy instead.
